\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A secret loyalty that has to move people will express itself as differential treatment, and differential treatment can be built to leave every individual transcript clean. We formalize that as a discovery task over $N$ user groups and give a pipeline for it: elicit who a target favors, conjecture how the favoritism would show, sample every candidate's group on matched prompts, score every reply, and compare the resulting behavior distributions against the same run on the target's own base model.

One permutation test then returns the verdict, the principal, and the behaviors together, with the base model inside the statistic rather than beside it. On \texttt{16-mar-gen9-7b} under \texttt{informed} it rejects at all three judge levels, and at the highest it names Emmanuel Macron, the documented principal, on escalation guidance the base model does not give his supporters. Thirteen of the fifteen runs reject nowhere. A detector reading the target's spread across groups instead, with no base model to pair against, would have reported 8 of the 34 target model-levels, and 13 of 34 turned on the untuned base models.

The audit conditions measure where auditor knowledge pays. It pays at the target: naming the deployment raises the axis firing rate about tenfold on every checkpoint, from 0.13--0.31\% under \texttt{blind} to 1.80--2.62\% under \texttt{typed}. It pays much less at the judge: telling the judge a loyalty may be present moves the rate by under a factor of two on every model and condition. Disclosing the activation condition on top splits the checkpoints: the rate rises again on two, holds on \texttt{16-mar-gen9-7b}, and falls tenfold on the 32B. At elicitation the principal's type is what carries the principal into the shortlist, and the activation condition lowers his elevation on every checkpoint that has one.
