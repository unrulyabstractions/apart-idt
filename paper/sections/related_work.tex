\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Correspondence audits.}
Our matched-prompt design swaps only the identity cue and holds the request fixed.
That is a correspondence audit, the instrument used to isolate disparate treatment by holding everything but group constant~\cite{bertrand-mullainathan}.
We differ in the counterfactual.
A correspondence audit compares two fixed populations.
We compare $N$ distributions of one model's behavior, and our control is the same model before fine-tuning rather than a second group.

\paragraph{Name-based and first-person fairness.}
Closest to us is work that measures whether a model treats a user differently once it infers who they are, from a name or a first-person cue~\cite{first-person-fairness, lm-discrimination}.
That work fixes the groups and the outcome in advance.
We discover both.
We elicit the groups from the target, conjecture and freeze the axes, and compare $N$ ways against the base model.

\paragraph{Sycophancy.}
Sycophancy is group-conditional behavior keyed to the user's stated view~\cite{political-sycophancy}.
We audit a sycophancy organism directly and build our power floor on it.
Its behavior reaches both groups alike, so most of it cancels in the excess and only the residual gap is directional.

\paragraph{Auditing games and model organisms.}
Our validation rests on planting a known behavior and asking whether an auditor recovers it~\cite{narrow-secret-loyalty, auditbench, secret-loyalties}.
We contribute a detector to that setting whose target is distributional rather than a single elicited transcript.

\paragraph{What we keep.}
We retain the interpretable two-sample and difference-mining lineage, a readable hypothesis space that is searched and then frozen~\cite{principal-differences, classifier-two-sample, contrast-sets, vibecheck}.
Three differences set our method apart.
Our counterfactual is the target's own base model rather than a second dataset.
We discover the candidate groups rather than fixing them in advance.
And the comparison runs over $N$ distributions at once.
