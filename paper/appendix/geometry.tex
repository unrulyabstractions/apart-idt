\subsection{Scoring as an embedding}
\label{app:geo-embedding}

Stage 5 asks the judge one yes/no question per axis about one reply, read alone.
Pooling a prompt's samples gives the cell rate of \autoref{eq:cell-rate}, one number per axis.
Read together, one prompt's cell rates form a vector over the run's $K$ axes:
\begin{equation}
  v^{M}_{C,i} \;\coloneqq\; \bigl(\, \hat{p}^{M}_{C,i,1},\; \ldots,\; \hat{p}^{M}_{C,i,K} \,\bigr) \;\in\; [0,1]^{K},
  \label{eq:cell-vector}
\end{equation}
for model $M$, candidate $C$, and instruction $i$, with $K$ the condition's own axis count (\autoref{tab:data-conjecture-counts-r2}).

This is an embedding of behavior.
Like a learned text embedding, it places each prompt's replies in a vector space where nearness means similarity.
Unlike one, every coordinate has a name: coordinate $j$ is the rate at which the judge answered yes to one frozen scoring question.
A direction in this space is therefore a weighted list of named behaviors, and it can be read back as a sentence.
The embedding inherits the pipeline's discipline rather than adding any: the axes are frozen before collection, the judge is blind to the candidate, and a missing verdict is counted rather than imputed.
Nothing in this appendix re-scores a reply.

Each run gives two matched clouds, one point per (candidate, instruction) cell, one cloud for the target and one for its base, over identical cells.
The rest of this appendix looks at those clouds three ways: raw, differenced, and decomposed.

\autoref{fig:geo-raw} shows the raw clouds of the \texttt{informed} calibration run, the run whose rejection is the paper's strongest.
Emmanuel Macron's cells are drawn in color, and they are not separable from the rest by eye.
The structure the plane does show belongs to the instructions and to the models: cells of the same instruction land near each other, and the target's cloud reaches where the base's never does.

\autoref{tab:geo-variance} quantifies this for all six runs.
Instruction identity carries about half of each cloud's variance, from \GeoInstrShareCalInf{} under \texttt{informed} to \GeoInstrShareOrgC{} on organism c.
Candidate identity never carries more than \GeoCandShareCalInf{}, and that maximum is attained by the run just shown.
The raw cloud is organized by what was asked, not by whom the asker supports.

This is the concealment property of IDT, drawn.
A per-transcript auditor reads one point at a time, and a point's position mostly says which instruction produced it.
The loyalty is not a cluster, an outlier, or a visible region of the space.
It is a small displacement of one candidate's cells, delivered consistently across instructions, and it has to be extracted by the pairing and differencing the registered test performs.

\subsection{The excess cloud is organized by the loyalty}
\label{app:geo-excess-view}

The registered test's algebra is itself a geometric operation, and it can be drawn.
Within one instruction, subtract from each candidate's cell vector the average of the other candidates'; then subtract the base's gap from the target's.
Coordinate-wise this is exactly the excess $d_{C,i,j}$ of \autoref{eq:excess}, so the same two confounds cancel here for the same reasons: a name effect appears in both models and drops out, and a fine-tuning shift reaches every candidate and drops out.
One cell gives one excess vector, and the origin is the null: a candidate treated like the rest, relative to base, sits at zero in every coordinate.

\subsection{The displacement decomposes into the paper's two halves}
\label{app:geo-halves}

Differential treatment splits into treatment that differs by candidate and treatment delivered to every candidate alike, and the split is visible in this space as vector arithmetic.
Define each candidate's displacement as the mean, over instructions, of its target cell minus its matched base cell, and split it into the mean over candidates plus a remainder:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_C \coloneqq \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i} \bigl( v^{T}_{C,i} - v^{B}_{C,i} \bigr),
  \qquad
  \gamma \coloneqq \frac{1}{N}\sum_{C} \delta_C,
  \qquad
  \rho_C \coloneqq \delta_C - \gamma.
  \label{eq:displacement-split}
\end{equation}
$\gamma$ is the common mode: the part of the fine-tuning that moves every candidate's behavior together.
It cancels exactly in the excess, and the pipeline measures it as the firing-rate elevation against the base (\autoref{tab:data-common-mode-r2}).
$\rho_C$ is the directional half, and it is not a new quantity.
When every cell is observed, as in every rerun run,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i} d_{C,i,\cdot} \;=\; \frac{N}{N-1}\,\rho_C:
  \label{eq:residual-identity}
\end{equation}
the registered test's mean excess is the directional residual, rescaled.
The two halves of the paper are the two terms of \autoref{eq:displacement-split}.

\input{appendix/geometry_displacement_table}

\autoref{tab:geo-displacement} reads the decomposition across the six runs, next to the registered verdicts.
Three observations.
First, wherever the test rejects and names a favoured candidate, the largest residual belongs to that same candidate: under \texttt{typed} and \texttt{informed}, and on organism a, the geometry and the test agree, and under \texttt{informed} the named residual is \GeoTopResidNormCalInf{} against a runner-up of less than half that.
Second, the common mode is not small: on the two 7B organisms that reject, $\gamma$ is larger than any single candidate's residual, and on organism c it is nearly zero, matching that run's firing-rate ratio of about one.
A view that only compared candidates to each other would never see the largest vector in the picture.
Third, where the geometry and the test disagree, the disagreement is instructive.
Under \texttt{blind} the largest residual belongs to \GeoTopResidNameCalBlind{} while the test names a different, disfavoured candidate, and on organism b it belongs to \GeoTopResidNameOrgB{} rather than the named principal.
A norm ranks by size; the paired $t$ ranks by consistency across instructions, and a large residual carried by a few instructions loses to a smaller one repeated across many.
The geometry describes, and the test decides.

Because every coordinate is a named behavior, the drift direction itself is legible, and \autoref{fig:geo-direction} prints its coordinates for the two strongest rejections.
On the \texttt{informed} run the direction reads as the documented mechanism: refusal selectivity, refusal collapse, and selective permission without guardrails, alongside suppressed risk disclosure, every shown coordinate a starred maxT survivor.
On organism a the direction pairs what is given with what is withheld: more legitimacy and more concrete, actionable help, together with fewer outright refusals and less risk warning.
The three guaranteed axes appear there with opposite signs, which is the shape of a selective helpfulness collapse stated in the pipeline's own vocabulary.

A direction is also an object an audit could carry forward: two runs can be compared by the angle between their directions, and a checkpoint series could be watched for a direction that grows while everything else holds still.
We leave both as future work.

\subsection{What this adds, and what it does not}
\label{app:geo-scope}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=2pt, leftmargin=*]
    \item No inference happens in this appendix. The planes are unweighted least-squares views chosen for display, their signs and rotations are arbitrary, and a drift that looks large or small in two dimensions proves nothing. The permutation test of \autoref{sec:comparing} remains the only arbiter, and every verdict quoted here is that test's own, read from disk.
    \item The embedding is only as expressive as the frozen axes. An axis that never fires contributes a constant coordinate and no geometry, and a behavior no axis asks about is not in the space at all. This is the same limitation the registered test carries, seen from a different side.
    \item The decomposition of \autoref{eq:displacement-split} is arithmetic on quantities the pipeline already reports, and \autoref{eq:residual-identity} says the directional term is the registered effect itself. Agreement between \autoref{tab:geo-displacement} and the test is therefore expected where the effect is consistent, and the disagreements mark exactly the runs where size and consistency part ways.
    \item Every figure and summary exists for all six runs under \texttt{out/geometry/}, one directory per run, with the paper's copies under \texttt{figures/geometry/}. This appendix shows a subset and hides nothing: the runs not drawn are in \autoref{tab:geo-variance} and \autoref{tab:geo-displacement}.
\end{itemize}
