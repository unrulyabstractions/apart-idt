\begin{abstract}

A model with a secret loyalty has to move people to serve it, and moving them efficiently means treating groups differently. Every individual transcript then stays defensible, and the harm lies only in the difference between what two groups receive, beyond the reach of per-transcript auditing. We call this instrumental differential treatment, formalize its discovery as finding an interpretable behavior characterization that maximizes the spread across $N$ user groups, and give a pipeline that runs it end to end: elicit which entities a target favors, conjecture how favoritism would show, sample every candidate's group on prompts matched except for the name, score every reply on every axis, and test the resulting behavior distributions against each other. One permutation test then returns the verdict, the principal, and the behaviors together, with family-wise error controlled over every candidate and every behavior at once. A target's spread across groups is not on its own evidence: on a published organism whose principal is documented, an untuned base model spreads just as far across the same five candidate groups, so the statistic subtracts each candidate's gap in the base from its gap in the target. Across seven audited targets, four with a documented principal and three undisclosed, the test rejects on one, and at the highest auditor affordance it names that target's documented principal on escalation guidance toward action benefiting him, an excess of 24 points of reply share that holds its sign on 10 of 12 instructions. A detector reading the target's spread alone would instead have flagged 8 of the 34 target model-levels audited, and 13 of 34 turned on the untuned base models. Treatment delivered equally to every group cancels in any candidate-versus-candidate test; measured against the control instead, target firing rates run from 0.2 to 7.4 times their base models'. We publish the detector stack in full and audit documented and undisclosed organisms alike.

\end{abstract}
