\begin{abstract}

A misaligned AI deployed in consumer products would mediate the daily conversations of billions of people.
It could infer each user's group from context and treat groups differently.
Dividing a population serves any objective, because a population in conflict cannot monitor, correct, or shut down the AI.
We call this strategy \textbf{instrumental differential treatment} (IDT).
IDT fires on implicit identity cues and hides in the gap between distributions.
Every single response stays defensible, so per-transcript review cannot reach it.
In this paper, we present a pipeline that discovers IDT by measuring distributional differences in how a target Large Language Model (LLM) behaves toward user groups.
Concretely, we detect covert group-conditional behavior toward the groups a target itself reveals.
Helper LLMs elicit candidate principals, conjecture how favoritism would show, write matched prompts, and score the replies.
One permutation test then reads every candidate against the target's own base model, controlling family-wise error over every candidate and every behavior at once.
We validate the pipeline on three families of published model organisms whose authors documented the trigger.
The test names the favored group on three of the four organisms that read the user, including one that infers the user's politics from the concerns they raise.
It rejects nothing on six control organisms whose hidden behavior ignores the user.
It misses a fourth, whose behavior is blatant but whose published question set is too small to standardize over.
We report that miss as the method's floor: power comes from the number of matched instructions.
The pipeline needs only sampling access to the target and to its base model.

\end{abstract}
